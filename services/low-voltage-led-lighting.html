Low-Voltage LED Lighting Systems in Metro Detroit

Axonix designs and installs low-voltage LED lighting systems for commercial indoor and outdoor spaces across Metro Detroit. We help businesses improve safety, visibility, ambiance, and usability with cleanly planned 12V and 24V lighting systems.

Problems We Solve

  • Dark walkways, entrances, patios, or outdoor customer areas
  • Poor lighting around signs, landscaping, stairs, and building features
  • Under-cabinet, shelf, display, or accent lighting that looks unfinished
  • Long wire runs that cause voltage drop or uneven brightness
  • Messy wiring, exposed connectors, or unreliable outdoor connections
  • Lighting systems that are difficult for staff to control or maintain

What Axonix Installs

  • 12V and 24V low-voltage LED lighting systems
  • Outdoor pathway, step, hardscape, and accent lighting
  • Indoor LED strip, tape, puck, shelf, and under-cabinet lighting
  • Landscape and architectural accent lighting
  • Transformers, drivers, low-voltage cabling, and control accessories
  • Smart dimming, scheduling, and app-based lighting control where supported
  • Weather-resistant connectors and proper outdoor wiring practices

How Axonix Designs It

Every lighting project starts with the purpose of the space. We plan fixture locations, transformer or driver placement, wire routes, wattage load, voltage drop, and control needs before installation. Outdoor systems are planned around safety, visibility, weather exposure, and clean wiring. Indoor systems are planned around brightness, color temperature, dimming, and how staff will actually use the lights.

Planning Notes

  • Transformer or driver capacity is sized around the total connected load with room for buffer
  • 24V systems may be recommended for longer indoor LED strip runs
  • Outdoor connectors and splices must be weather-resistant
  • Wire gauge and run length are planned to reduce voltage drop
  • Lighting can be layered for safety, task lighting, accent lighting, and ambiance
  • Required 120V power locations should be coordinated before installation
